Consensus without a chairmanProblem

Nodes see transactions in different orders. Who decides the one official history? A competition: whoever burns the most computing power proposes the next block, and everyone checks it. This is Nakamoto consensus.

2 / 8

Proof-of-workPoW

A block is valid only if its header hash, as a number, is below a target. Miners vary the nonce and hash trillions of times to find one.

valid block ⇔ hash(header) ≤ target

Hard to find, instant to verify — so cheating is pointless.

3 / 8

The reward and the supplyReward

Miners earn the block reward = subsidy + fees. The subsidy halves every 210,000 blocks, curving the supply toward 21 million.

4 / 8

nBits and retargetingTarget

The 256-bit target is packed into 4 bytes (nBits): target = mantissa × 256^(exp−3). Every 2,016 blocks the target adjusts toward 10-minute blocks, clamped to a factor of 4.

5 / 8

Longest-chain ruleForks

If two blocks appear at once the chain forks; nodes follow the branch with the most accumulated work. The other is abandoned.
