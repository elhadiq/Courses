The key idea: coins are UTXOsConcept

Bitcoin has no account balances. The ledger is a pile of individual chunks of money called outputs.

  • Like bills in your pocket: a $20, a $5, two $1s — discrete objects.
  • A chunk you've received and not yet spent is a UTXO (Unspent Transaction Output).
  • Your “balance” is just the sum of your UTXOs.

The fee = inputs − outputsFees

A transaction never states its fee. Whatever is left over goes to the miner.

fee = Σ(inputs) − Σ(outputs)

   = 100000 − (60000 + 39000) = 1000 sat

Forget an output and the “missing” value becomes a huge fee. That's why change matters.
5 / 10

Change: getting your money backChange

An input must be spent in full. Alice's only UTXO is 100,000 sat but the coffee is 60,000 — so her wallet pays the leftover back to herself.

change = inputs − amount − fee

      = 100000 − 60000 − 1000 = 39000 sat

The one rule: no money from nothingConsensus

valid ⇔ Σ(outputs) ≤ Σ(inputs)  AND  every output > 0

The one exception is the coinbase transaction that pays the miner brand-new coins (Section 12).

Confirmations = safetyFinality

  • A broadcast transaction waits in miners' mempools.
  • A miner packs it into a block by solving proof-of-work.
  • Once accepted, the payment has 1 confirmation.
  • Each further block makes reversal exponentially harder.
Coffee: 1 confirmation is fine. A large transfer: wait for ~6.
