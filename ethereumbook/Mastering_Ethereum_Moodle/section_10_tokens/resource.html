Tokens

By the end of this lesson you will understand the ERC-20 fungible-token standard, its approve/transferFrom workflow, decimals, and how ERC-721 and ERC-1155 extend the idea to unique and multi-token assets.

What you will be able to do

  • Explain what a token standard is and why fungibility matters.
  • List the required ERC-20 functions and describe the approve/transferFrom flow.
  • Convert between a token's raw integer amount and its human-readable value using decimals.
  • Contrast ERC-20, ERC-721 (NFT), and ERC-1155 (multitoken).

1. What a token standard is

A token is just an entry in a smart contract that tracks who owns how much of something. A token standard is a shared interface — a fixed set of functions — that every compatible token implements, so that wallets, exchanges, and other contracts can work with any of them the same way. The first and by far most common standard is ERC-20, proposed in 2015 (it happened to be GitHub issue #20).

ERC-20 is a standard for fungible tokens: every unit is identical and interchangeable, exactly like ordinary money — one of your dollars is as good as any other. The contract just needs to track balances.

2. The ERC-20 interface

Every ERC-20 token must provide these functions and two events:

FunctionWhat it does
totalSupplyHow many tokens exist in total.
balanceOf(addr)The token balance of an address.
transfer(to, amt)Send your own tokens to someone.
approve(spender, amt)Authorize someone to spend up to amt of your tokens.
transferFrom(from, to, amt)A spender moves approved tokens on your behalf.
allowance(owner, spender)How much the spender is still allowed to move.

The approve / transferFrom pair is the clever part. Because a contract cannot start a transaction, you first approve a spender (say, a marketplace) for an amount; the spender then calls transferFrom to pull tokens up to that allowance. Each transfer reduces the remaining allowance — and a spender can never move more than they were approved for. Computing that remaining allowance is one of the exercises.

3. Decimals

ERC-20 balances are stored as plain integers, so tokens use an optional decimals value to place the decimal point for display — much like wei and ether. If a token has 2 decimals, a stored balance of 1000 means 10 tokens; with the common 18 decimals, one whole token is stored as 1000000000000000000. To show whole tokens you divide the raw amount by 10decimals:

whole tokens = raw ÷ 10decimals
raw = 1000, decimals = 2 → 10 whole tokens

The token also carries an optional name (e.g. “US dollars”) and symbol (e.g. “USD”). Converting a raw amount to whole tokens is the second exercise.

4. Beyond fungible: ERC-721 and ERC-1155

Not everything is interchangeable. Two other standards extend the idea:

StandardKindIdea
ERC-20FungibleInterchangeable units; track balances.
ERC-721Non-fungible (NFT)Each token is unique, identified by a uint256 ID mapped to an owner. Art, collectibles, deeds.
ERC-1155Multi-tokenOne contract manages many token types (fungible and not) with efficient batch transfers.

ERC-721 gives each token a unique ID and an ownerOf lookup, often with a metadata URI (frequently on IPFS) pointing to its image and attributes. ERC-1155 lets a game, say, hold fungible gold, unique swords, and stackable potions in a single contract. Classifying a token by its kind is the third exercise.

Check your understanding

  1. What does “fungible” mean? (Answer: every unit is identical and interchangeable.)
  2. Why do you need approve before transferFrom? (Answer: to authorize a spender up to an allowance.)
  3. With 2 decimals, what does a raw balance of 1000 represent? (Answer: 10 tokens.)
  4. Which standard is used for one-of-a-kind NFTs? (Answer: ERC-721.)
