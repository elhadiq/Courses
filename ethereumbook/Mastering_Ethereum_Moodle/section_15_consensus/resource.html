Consensus

  • Define safety, finality, and liveness, and how forking balances them.
  • Contrast proof of work with proof of stake and its intrinsic penalties.
  • Explain slots, epochs, validators, attestations, and slashing.
  • Identify the two components of Gasper: a fork-choice rule and a finality gadget.

1. Safety, finality, and liveness

A consensus protocol must juggle three ideas. Safety means the network never disagrees about history — no double-spends, one shared view. Finality puts safety into practice: a point past which a transaction is irreversible. Liveness means the chain keeps making progress — new blocks always come, eventually.

You cannot fully guarantee both safety and liveness under all conditions, so Ethereum favours liveness when the network is chaotic, using forking. Blocks can briefly form a tree rather than a line; the protocol's fork-choice rule picks the branch most likely to become canonical. If a node followed a losing branch, it must reorg back to the winning one — disruptive, so protocols minimise it. Finality is what tames this flexibility with hard safety guarantees.

2. Proof of work vs proof of stake

Ethereum began on proof of work (its algorithm was Ethash), where miners spend electricity to secure the chain; the punishment for cheating is extrinsic — wasted energy. Since The Merge (September 2022) Ethereum uses proof of stake. Anyone who locks up ether can become a validator; they take turns proposing and voting on blocks, weighted by their stake, earning small rewards for honest participation. The punishment here is intrinsic — losing staked ether. A key advantage: because the validator set is known, the network can tell when a majority has voted, which enables finality.

Becoming an Ethereum validator requires depositing 32 ETH. Minor mistakes (being offline) cost small penalties; being provably malicious — like signing two conflicting blocks — triggers slashing, a severe loss of stake.

Deciding whether a deposit is enough to become a validator is one of the exercises.

3. Time: slots and epochs

Ethereum's proof of stake runs on a strict clock. Time is divided into slots of 12 seconds, and 32 slots make one epoch. In each slot exactly one validator is chosen to propose a block; across each epoch every validator casts exactly one attestation — a vote that carries both a head-of-chain vote (for the fork choice) and checkpoint votes (for finality).

1 slot = 12 seconds  ·  1 epoch = 32 slots
epoch of a slot = slot ÷ 32 (integer)

Converting a slot number to its epoch is the first exercise.

4. Gasper: LMD-GHOST + Casper FFG

Ethereum's proof-of-stake protocol is called Gasper, and it is the fusion of two mechanisms:

ComponentJob
LMD-GHOSTThe fork-choice rule: pick the head of the chain from validators' latest votes (liveness).
Casper FFGThe finality gadget: justify and finalize checkpoints so history becomes irreversible (safety).

Together they give Ethereum both a way to keep progressing under bad conditions and a way to lock in irreversible finality under good ones. Matching a consensus principle (agreement, progress, irreversibility) to its name is the third exercise.
