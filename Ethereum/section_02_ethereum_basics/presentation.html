Ether and weiUnits

Ethereum is the system; ether is the currency. Internally, all values are integers counted in wei.

1 ether = 1 × 10^18 wei = 1,000,000,000,000,000,000 wei

Wallets and controlKeys

  • A wallet holds your keys and sends transactions for you.
  • Rule of thumb: one private key = one account.
  • Control your keys → control your funds and contracts.
Lose the private key and the funds are gone forever — no one can restore them.
Only install wallet software you trust; never share the key itself.

A first contract: the faucetExample

A faucet gives a little ether to anyone who asks and can be refilled. Written in Solidity, compiled to EVM bytecode.

Its withdrawal enforces two checks before paying:

  • request must not exceed the per-withdrawal limit;
  • request must not exceed the current balance.
balance=1000, request=100, limit=100 → pay, left 900 balance=1000, request=200, limit=100 → reject (too big) balance=50, request=100, limit=100 → reject (no funds)

Why this mattersBig picture

The faucet is tiny, but it shows the whole shape of Ethereum development: a contract is code that holds ether and enforces rules automatically, with no human in the loop.

Ether meters computation; accounts hold value; contracts encode the rules. Everything else in the book builds on these three ideas.

Recap

1 ETH = 10^18 wei. A wallet holds your keys; controlling them is your responsibility. EOAs have keys and start transactions; contract accounts have code and only react. A faucet contract enforces a per-withdrawal limit and a balance check before paying out.
