4.1 What CI and CD actually mean

The terms are used loosely; the distinctions matter because they imply different amounts of risk.

TermMeansThe hard part
Continuous IntegrationEveryone merges into main at least daily, and every merge is automatically built and tested.Not the tooling — the discipline of small, frequent merges and a test suite you actually trust.
Continuous DeliveryEvery commit on main produces a deployable artefact. Deploying is a button someone presses.Making the artefact genuinely deployable, every time, with no manual steps.
Continuous DeploymentEvery green commit on main goes to production automatically.Test coverage and observability good enough that nobody needs to look first.
Why it exists Manual builds are unrepeatable and manual test runs get skipped under pressure — exactly when they matter most. A pipeline makes the safe path the only path: you cannot merge without the tests passing, so nobody has to remember to run them.

The most important property of a pipeline is not speed but trust. A suite that fails randomly teaches the team to re-run it and ignore it, which is worse than having no suite at all, because it costs time and provides false assurance.
