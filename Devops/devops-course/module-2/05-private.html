2.5 Private ranges, NAT and why your laptop is invisible

Three IPv4 ranges are reserved for private networks. They are not routable on the internet, so everyone reuses them:

RangeCIDRWhere you meet it
10.0.0.0 – 10.255.255.25510.0.0.0/8Cloud VPCs, Kubernetes pod networks
172.16.0.0 – 172.31.255.255172.16.0.0/12Docker's default bridge — usually 172.17.0.0/16
192.168.0.0 – 192.168.255.255192.168.0.0/16Home and office LANs
127.0.0.0 – 127.255.255.255127.0.0.0/8Loopback — never leaves the machine

NAT (Network Address Translation) is what lets many private addresses share one public one. Your router rewrites the source address and port of outbound packets, remembers the mapping, and reverses it on the way back.

Why NAT explains a whole class of problems NAT is directional. Outbound connections work because the router created a mapping when you initiated them. Inbound connections have no mapping, so they fail — which is why you can browse the web from your laptop but nobody can browse to your laptop without explicit port forwarding.

Docker's -p 8080:80 is exactly this: a port-forwarding rule from the host into the container's private network. Kubernetes NodePort is the same idea again. Once you see NAT, you see it everywhere in this course.
