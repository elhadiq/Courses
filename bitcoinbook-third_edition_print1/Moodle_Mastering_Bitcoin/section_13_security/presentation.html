Control of keys is control of coinsPrinciple

Everything reduces to: whoever controls the private keys controls the coins. No support line, no reset. Two risks: losing keys and leaking keys.

Golden rule. Never type your recovery phrase into a website, never photograph it, never store it in the cloud. Paper or steel, kept physically safe.
2 / 7

Preventing costly typosChecksums

Addresses carry a checksum: the last 4 bytes are the first 4 bytes of the payload's double-SHA256. A single wrong character fails the check and the address is rejected.

valid ⇔ SHA256(SHA256(payload))[:4] == checksum

Removing single points of failureRedundancy

Multisig spreads keys across locations (an m-of-n tolerates losing n−m). Cold storage keeps savings keys offline, away from malware.
