Second-Layer Applications

The base blockchain is secure but slow and limited. This section shows how layers built on top — payment channels and the Lightning Network — add speed and scale without weakening the base.

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why a second layer is needed and what “layer 2” means.
  • Describe a payment channel: open, update off-chain, close.
  • Explain how channels connect into the Lightning Network and how routing fees work.
  • Understand the HTLC mechanism that makes multi-hop payments safe.

1. Why build on top?

Every on-chain transaction must be stored and verified by every node forever (Section 3). That is what makes Bitcoin secure — and also what limits it to a modest number of transactions per second. Buying coffee twice a day for millions of people simply will not fit on-chain. The solution is not to change the base layer but to build a second layer on top: move most activity off-chain, and use the blockchain only as the final judge when needed.

2. Payment channels

A payment channel lets two people transact many times while touching the blockchain only twice. It has three phases:

  1. Open (funding). The two parties lock some bitcoin into a shared 2-of-2 multisig output with one on-chain transaction.
  2. Update (off-chain). They pay each other by exchanging signed new balance sheets (called commitment transactions) — instantly, privately, for free, as often as they like. Only the latest agreed balance matters.
  3. Close (settlement). Either party publishes the final balance on-chain, and each receives what they are owed.

Each update just moves value between the two sides while keeping the total (the channel capacity) constant. If Emma and Fabian open a channel with 100,000 sat all on Emma's side, and Emma pays Fabian 30,000 sat, the new balance is Emma 70,000 / Fabian 30,000 — no blockchain transaction required.

3. The Lightning Network

Channels are useful for two parties, but you cannot open one with everyone. The Lightning Network connects channels into a web: if Alice has a channel with Bob, and Bob with Carol, then Alice can pay Carol through Bob. Payments hop across a route of existing channels, so you can pay someone you have no direct channel with.

Intermediate nodes like Bob charge a small routing fee for forwarding. A fee has two parts: a fixed base fee plus a proportional part measured in parts-per-million (ppm) of the amount. Amounts on Lightning are counted in millisatoshis (msat, thousandths of a satoshi) for precision.

routing_fee = base_msat + amount_msat × ppm / 1,000,000

4. Making multi-hop safe: HTLCs

How does Alice pay Carol through Bob without trusting Bob to pass the money along? The trick is a Hashed Timelock Contract (HTLC). Carol picks a secret and shares its hash. The payment is set up so that each hop can only claim its money by revealing the secret (the preimage) — and Carol reveals it only when paid. A built-in timeout guarantees that if the secret never appears, everyone gets refunded. So either the whole route completes, or it safely unwinds.

With the preimage before the timeout → claim the payment.  After the timeout with no preimage → refund the sender.
